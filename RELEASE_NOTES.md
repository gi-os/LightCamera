## Roll v2.4 — smaller stamps, and a viewer that turns and zooms

**The stamps were far too big**

- **Quartz** is about a third the size, sized off the long edge rather than the short one, with
  digits that are tall and narrow instead of square, thinner bars, and a notch at every joint so it
  reads as seven separate segments rather than a welded block. A date back's display is a couple of
  centimetres of LCD reflected into the corner of a 35mm frame — small.
- **Camcorder** is smaller too, and the keyline is a hairline rather than a slab: it was one pixel of
  video, not a stroke you'd notice.

**The viewer turns with the phone**

Hold the phone on its side and the photograph fills the long edge, chrome and all. The window stays
locked to portrait — unlocking it would let the *viewfinder* reflow, which is the one thing that must
never happen — so the viewer is rotated against the phone instead, off the accelerometer, with sixty
degrees of hysteresis so a picture never reorients itself while you are looking at it.

**And it zooms**

Pinch to six times, drag to move about, bounded so the picture can't be dragged off the screen. The
pager keeps horizontal swipes until you're zoomed in, at which point panning wins — and a tap, which
normally hides the chrome, zooms back out instead. Zoom resets when you move to another photograph.

**Scrolling direction**

Now the same sense as the roll grid you came from: a turn up walks towards the newest photograph. It
had been flipped to match a list's scroll direction, which left the two screens disagreeing — worse
than either choice on its own.
